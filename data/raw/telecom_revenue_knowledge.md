# Telecom Revenue Assurance Knowledge Base

## Prepaid Recharge Revenue

Prepaid recharge revenue represents value added by prepaid subscribers through recharge channels such as vouchers, electronic reloads, banking applications, dealer systems, and digital payment platforms.

A sudden reduction in prepaid recharge revenue may result from a genuine decline in subscriber activity, recharge platform outages, dealer network issues, payment gateway failures, delayed transaction feeds, incorrect product configuration, or incomplete loading of recharge records into downstream revenue assurance systems.

Revenue assurance analysts should compare recharge transaction counts and values against historical baselines. They should also compare source-system totals with mediation, billing, and revenue assurance records to determine whether the reduction represents a genuine business change or a data-processing problem.

## Usage Revenue

Usage revenue is generated when subscribers consume chargeable telecommunications services such as voice, SMS, and mobile data.

Unexpected changes in usage revenue may be caused by traffic changes, rating errors, incorrect tariff configuration, missing usage records, mediation failures, billing failures, promotional changes, or network incidents.

Analysts should compare traffic volumes with charged amounts. A major increase in traffic without a corresponding increase in revenue may indicate incorrect rating, zero-rated traffic, missing charging events, or an unintended tariff configuration.

## Roaming Revenue

Outbound roaming revenue is generated when subscribers use services while connected to foreign mobile networks. Roaming charging and settlement may involve network usage records, billing systems, TAP records, roaming agreements, and settlement processes.

Differences between roaming usage and billed revenue can result from missing TAP records, delayed TAP files, incorrect roaming tariffs, currency conversion problems, rejected records, duplicate records, or incorrect mapping between network events and billing systems.

Revenue assurance controls should reconcile roaming usage records with billing records and settlement information and investigate unexplained differences.

## Interconnect Revenue

Interconnect revenue is associated with traffic exchanged between telecommunications operators.

Revenue leakage may occur when interconnect traffic is missing, incorrectly classified, incorrectly rated, or excluded from settlement. Incorrect routing information and incomplete CDR processing may also create discrepancies.

Revenue assurance teams commonly reconcile switch or network traffic against mediation, billing, and interconnect settlement records.

## Revenue Anomaly Investigation

A revenue anomaly is an unexpected deviation from normal revenue behaviour.

An anomaly does not automatically represent revenue leakage. It may result from seasonality, subscriber behaviour, public holidays, promotions, network outages, system failures, product launches, pricing changes, or data quality problems.

An analyst should therefore investigate both business and technical explanations before classifying an anomaly as revenue leakage.
